#### 4.1.11 Stretch analytics and adversarial scenario

**BLUF:** Stretch lanes add evidence about the score, the engine, and the defenses, and each merges only when green so none can endanger the core demo.

**Frame**

- **Who:** The owner and judges who want proof beyond the demo.
- **What:** Adversarial scenario, score backtest, Rust matching core, ML comparison, and the qlib strategy (DES-GM-ADV, DES-GM-BT, DES-GM-RUST, DES-GM-ML, DES-GM-QUANT).
- **Why:** Claims of speed, predictive value, and robustness need measured evidence.
- **How:** Offline commands with their own request budgets of at most 5 per minute, never run during the recording.
- **When:** Stretch assembly S18 no later than Sat 22:00 CDT; lanes not green are dropped.
- **Where:** `adversary.py`, `backtest.py`, `rust/matching_core/`, `ml.py`, and `quant_strategy.py`.

The adversarial scenario drives three attacks through the public API: an
over-capacity spot sell, a replayed Idempotency-Key, and a burst of 50 orders
in 1 s; each is rejected without state change and shown as an anomaly
(AC-GM-ADV-01). The backtest reports the Spearman rank correlation of
zone-hour scores against realized RT SPP over at least 7 days, with n and the
date range (AC-GM-BT-01). The Rust PyO3 core must pass the shared parity
suite, and it is enabled only if a 100,000-order seeded benchmark shows higher
throughput (AC-GM-PERF-01). The ML lane trains a gradient-boosted model on at
least 28 days and compares its Spearman value with the deterministic score on
the next 7 days, with feature importances; no model is served
(AC-GM-ML-01). The qlib strategy uses pyqlib 0.9.7 from the optional `quant`
extra, trades with its own key, and never enters the runtime image unless its
lane merges (AC-GM-QUANT-01).
