4. **R3 — DST, signed prices and rounding need executable conventions.**
Problem: a Texas day need not contain 96 quarters; current `ercot._hour()` does not resolve repeated-hour flags. Integer cents alone leaves fractional-energy settlement and negative prices ambiguous.
Exact replacement for R3: “D is the America/Chicago local calendar day, represented by half-open UTC intervals between its two local midnights: 92, 96 or 100 quarters. Parse hour-ending, interval numbering and repeated-hour flags according to each pinned source schema; distinguish both fall-back hours and reject unresolved/duplicate keys. Use kWh and AC kW per asset, MWh/MW for grid series, and retain signed source $/MWh prices without live-order price caps. Monetary contribution in cents is kWh × $/MWh ÷ 10. Compute with decimal arithmetic; round HALF_EVEN once per strategy/day ledger component, and sum those posted integer-cent components to net_value_cents. Do not round prices to cents/kWh first. Energy conservation tolerance is 0.000001 kWh per asset step; monetary reconciliation is exact. Test negative prices, sub-cent amounts, midnight/hour-ending 24 and both DST transitions.”

5. **R4, C2 — Efficiency is defensible, but the model lacks a meter boundary and load balance.**
Problem: sqrt(round-trip efficiency) is a valid simplifying convention only when charge/discharge powers are AC-side and SoC is stored DC energy. Existing provider capacity is SoC minus reserve; it does not enforce power, efficiency or depletion across deliveries.
Exact replacement for R4: “For each asset, E is stored DC kWh; C and D are nonnegative average AC charging/discharging kW over h hours; eta_c = eta_d = sqrt(eta_round_trip), with 0 < eta_round_trip <= 1. E_next = E + eta_c*C*h − D*h/eta_d. Enforce C <= max_charge_kw, D <= max_discharge_kw, C*D = 0, 0 <= reserve <= capacity, and reserve <= initial_E <= capacity; reject nonfinite or invalid inputs. Bound charging by headroom and discharge by stored energy above reserve after all outstanding reservations. Commercial discharge never crosses reserve. hold/preserve_backup issue no new charge/discharge or offers and do not cancel existing commitments; preserve_backup suppresses new offers. Record attempted reserve violations and actual breach asset-intervals separately; an actual breach invalidates the run rather than being silently clamped. Household load L is explicitly simulated AC kW: grid import = max(L+C−D,0), export = max(D−L−C,0). Discharge serving load and export are disjoint portions of D. No implicit solar, automatic household depletion, standby loss or degradation is modelled; disclose these assumptions. Test a 1-kWh AC charge/discharge cycle returns eta_round_trip kWh, power/headroom clipping, reserve limits and the AC/DC balance.”

6. **C2 — Named policies and reasons are not yet reproducible contracts.**

Exact replacement for C2's Decision/policy clauses: “Decision includes asset_id, decision_time, action, nonnegative AC kw, delivery_start/end, reason, policy_version/config and inputs with name/value/unit/source/interval_start/end/published_at/available_at/quality. Reasons distinguish requested, accepted and delivered quantities. For charge, the window is the current quarter; offer_flex targets the next quarter; hold/preserve_backup have kw=0. FixedSchedule charges at feasible maximum during 00:00–06:00 Central and offers feasible maximum for delivery during 17:00–21:00. PriceBased uses the zone's full, already-published D DAM vector: nearest-rank 25th/75th percentiles; charge when current-hour price <= Q25, offer when next-quarter delivery-hour price >= Q75, otherwise hold; if Q25 >= Q75, hold. Offer takes precedence if both predicates hold. Missing/ineligible DAM means hold with a reason. EsrInformed follows PriceBased, but may also offer when latest eligible RT price >= Q75 and the latest two complete eligible 15-minute ESR bins show decreasing charging magnitude; label this a simulated price/trend heuristic, not an ERCOT scarcity declaration. Missing ESR/RT inputs produce an explicitly recorded PriceBased fallback. Feasibility and existing commitments always override policy requests. Policies shall not access global signal stores, wall clock or RNG. Simulated household profiles shall have declared kW units and timestamps; population activity weights shall not be relabelled as measured consumption.”

7. **C3 — ‘Simple market rule (documented)’ leaves acceptance and commitments undefined.**
Problem: unlimited automatic acceptance can make every offer profitable; sharing one market between compared strategies changes their opportunities. Hour-only reservations can promise the same stored energy repeatedly.
Exact replacement for the commitment/market clause: “Use a labelled simulated peak-flex procurement programme, not ERCOT clearing. For each zone and each quarter starting 17:00–21:00 Central, demand is 25% of that zone's initial fleet rated AC discharge kW, fixed for all strategy copies; outside that window it is zero. At t, offers target [t+15min,t+30min) wholly within D. Accept valid online offers pro rata up to that fixed demand, with deterministic asset-ID allocation of any 0.000001-kWh rounding residual; reject excess/invalid quantities explicitly. A strategy runs against its own identical procurement schedule, not competing strategies. Reserve accepted AC kWh divided by eta_d immediately against the asset's stored energy, subtracting every undelivered reservation across delivery windows; do not collateralize with hoped-for charging. Current commitments have priority and prevent simultaneous charging. Persist immutable accepted quantity and delivery window; deliver physically feasible energy once, then release its reservation once. Positive shortfall > 0.000001 kWh is a failed commitment, counted once with cause and shortfall_kwh. Unaccepted offers earn nothing and are not failures. Refuse commitments beyond day end. The bonus is $10/MWh delivered, and shortfall penalty is $100/MWh undelivered, both explicitly synthetic and fixed before comparisons.”
Verification: under/oversubscribed allocation, zero demand, sequential offers exhausting energy, repeated settlement and a post-acceptance outage have exact expected outcomes.

8. **C3 scoreboard — Net value can double-count costs/revenue and reward free opening inventory.**
Problem: ‘energy arbitrage’ often already subtracts charging cost; adding flexibility payments can pay twice for the same energy. Adding only terminal SoC value gives hold a windfall. Final-price marking can dominate ranking and is not executable liquidation.
Exact replacement for the scoreboard clause: “The ledger shall expose energy_value_cents, charging_cost_cents, flexibility_bonus_cents, shortfall_penalty_cents, opening_energy_value_cents and terminal_energy_value_cents. For each asset/quarter use its load-zone RT SPP: energy value = delivered AC kWh × SPP/10; charging cost = actual AC charging kWh × SPP/10; bonus = delivered committed kWh × 10/10; penalty = shortfall_kwh × 100/10. Costs and energy values retain their signs, including negative-price charging credits and discharge losses. Energy value includes either avoided baseline import or exported energy exactly once; the explicit simplifying assumption is symmetric wholesale valuation, not a retail tariff. No separate ‘arbitrage’ credit, payment at acceptance, or payment for undelivered quantities is added. Let v be max(0, mean of the zone's eligible D DAM prices)) fixed before replay. Opening/terminal inventory marks are eta_d × max(E−reserve,0) × v/10, summed across assets and separately rounded; reserve inventory is excluded from commercial valuation. net_value = energy_value − charging_cost + bonus − penalty + terminal_mark − opening_mark. Also show cash_net excluding both marks; marks are noncash comparison values, not guaranteed liquidation proceeds. A no-action run scores zero. Report requested/accepted/delivered/shortfall kWh, start/end SoC, observed minimum SoC, configured reserve, actual breach count and attempted violations. Unsettled commitments or missing required prices invalidate ranking, rather than posting zero. Negative results remain visible.”
Verification: hand-reconcile hold, one lossy cycle, partial delivery, negative SPP and identical actions split across assets; ledger components sum exactly and no energy receives duplicate credit.

It is not a household grid blackout. feed_interrupt stops delivery of new observations for that source; it does not erase already-known DAM prices, physical dispatch or commitments. Previously known actual observations expire after 30 minutes from interval_end and are then missing; DAM remains valid for its published delivery day. Missing actual data invokes the C2 fallback, never zero. On recovery, expose only observations then available under R2; never rewrite past actions or liabilities. Apply current-time disruptions before information views/decisions and physical delivery; strategies cannot inspect future windows. UI intervention creates a new immutable scenario from the same initial state and preserves the baseline for comparison.”
Verification: outages beginning at delivery, ending at delivery, overlapping windows, lost ESR versus lost price feeds and post-recovery replay each have deterministic, distinct results.

11. **C4, C6 — Flexibility, backup hours and MW scaling need a duration and starting SoC.**
