# GridMarket — Hackathon Intent and Build Specification

**Working title:** GridMarket  
**Event:** Base / AITX Talent Hackathon  
**Status:** Weekend MVP intent  
**Primary objective:** Build a provider-neutral, API-first simulated market for distributed energy flexibility using real ERCOT market data.

---

## 1. Background — Why I Am Building This

Distributed batteries are becoming active grid resources rather than passive backup devices. Companies such as Base coordinate residential batteries so stored energy can respond to grid conditions, wholesale prices, reliability needs, and periods of scarcity.

The idea behind GridMarket is to explore the next layer above that infrastructure:

> **What if customers could participate in a market for the economic value of their battery flexibility, and what if that market were open to humans, algorithms, AI agents, and multiple energy providers through one common platform?**

The hackathon is a good environment to test this because the concept combines:

- real ERCOT market and grid data;
- distributed-energy orchestration;
- customer-facing energy economics;
- predictive analytics;
- algorithmic and AI-assisted trading;
- platform APIs;
- provider integrations;
- security and adversarial resilience.

The goal is **not** to recreate ERCOT, become a retail electric provider, or launch a regulated financial exchange during the hackathon. The goal is to build a realistic simulation that demonstrates how such a platform could work.

The platform should answer four questions:

1. Can real ERCOT data create a believable simulated energy-flexibility market?
2. Can customers use predictions to decide when future flexibility is likely to become more valuable?
3. Can multiple battery providers expose their customers to the same market through a normalized adapter?
4. Can humans, algorithms, and AI agents all trade through the same safe API?

---

## 2. Product Intent

GridMarket is a **simulated distributed-energy exchange and predictive-analysis platform**.

It allows simulated energy customers to:

- buy and sell flexibility credits;
- trade simulated future flexibility contracts;
- view predictions for energy scarcity and future flexibility value;
- automate trading with rules, algorithms, or AI agents;
- participate through Base-like energy providers connected through a provider adapter;
- test strategies through a public sandbox API.

Real ERCOT data drives market conditions. Battery assets, customer accounts, trades, settlement, and provider fleets are simulated for the hackathon.

---

## 3. Core Product Thesis

Energy flexibility has different value depending on:

- location;
- time;
- expected demand;
- available generation;
- generator outages;
- renewable availability;
- transmission congestion;
- current and expected market prices.

A customer with a battery therefore has an economic resource whose future value can change throughout the day.

GridMarket makes that value:

1. **visible** through predictive analytics;
2. **tradable** through spot and future flexibility markets;
3. **programmable** through an API;
4. **portable** across energy providers through adapters;
5. **safer** through policy, risk, and security controls.

## 4. Simulation Model

### 4.1 Sandbox accounts

Every generated user account begins with:

**$1,000 of simulated trading value.**

In the UI this may be displayed as **$1,000 demo credits**, but internally it should be modeled as simulated cash so it is not confused with energy Flex Credits.

Each account tracks:

- simulated cash balance;
- Flex Credit inventory;
- open orders;
- future positions;
- realized P&L;
- unrealized P&L;
- battery assets;
- reserved battery capacity;
- transaction history;
- API credentials;
- risk-policy settings.

No real money is used.

### 4.2 Simulated battery assets

Each account may have one or more simulated batteries.

Example:

```yaml
battery_id: bat_001
provider: base-sim
zone: LZ_HOUSTON
capacity_kwh: 20
state_of_charge_kwh: 15
minimum_reserve_kwh: 8
available_flexibility_kwh: 7
max_charge_kw: 5
max_discharge_kw: 5
online: true
```

The market must never allow committed capacity to exceed verified available capacity.

---

## 5. Flex Credits

A **Flex Credit** is a simulated unit representing the commitment of battery flexibility for a specific delivery interval.

For the hackathon:

> **1 Flex Credit = 1 kWh of verified battery flexibility for a defined interval.**

A Flex Credit is **not** a Renewable Energy Certificate (REC), cryptocurrency, or claim that a specific electron moves from one house to another.

Example:

```text
Seller:
  Available battery flexibility: 8 kWh
  Minimum reserve: 40%

Offer:
  Sell 5 Flex Credits
  Delivery: 6:00–7:00 PM
  Minimum price: $0.20 / credit
```

---

## 6. Spot Flexibility Market

The spot market allows users to buy or sell Flex Credits for a near-term delivery interval.

Initial market behavior:

- limit orders;
- buy and sell sides;
- price-time priority;
- asset-capacity reservation before accepting a sell order;
- atomic matching;
- append-only trade ledger;
- simulated settlement.

Example:

```text
BUY  4 FLEX-HOUSTON-18:00 @ <= $0.24
SELL 4 FLEX-HOUSTON-18:00 @ >= $0.21

Market clears at the configured matching price.
```

## 7. Simulated Future Flexibility Market

Users may trade contracts representing future flexibility value.

Example product:

```yaml
symbol: FLEX-LZ_HOUSTON-2026-09-26-18
zone: LZ_HOUSTON
delivery_start: 2026-09-26T18:00
delivery_end: 2026-09-26T19:00
unit: 1_kwh_flexibility
settlement: simulated_cash
```

The MVP should use a simplified settlement model.

The platform must clearly state that these are **simulated future/forward flexibility contracts**, not real regulated commodity futures.

MVP functions:

- create future products for upcoming intervals;
- buy/sell positions;
- show market price;
- calculate unrealized P&L;
- settle at expiration against a defined simulated reference value;
- enforce position limits and account balance constraints.

Do **not** add options, leverage, sophisticated margin, or real-money settlement during the weekend MVP.

## 8. ERCOT Data Layer

Real ERCOT data should drive the simulation.

### MVP data signals

1. Real-Time Settlement Point Prices (SPP)
2. Day-Ahead Settlement Point Prices
3. Current/load forecast data
4. Wind forecasts
5. Solar forecasts
6. Resource outage capacity
7. Transmission congestion / binding constraints
8. Shadow prices

### Why outages matter

ERCOT publishes **Hourly Resource Outage Capacity** aggregated by Load Zone for the next 168 hours and updates the report hourly.

This can become a forward scarcity signal:

```text
More expected outages
+ high expected demand
+ weak renewable forecast
= potentially higher future scarcity/value
```

### Why congestion matters

ERCOT publishes public SCED shadow prices and binding transmission constraints.

Congestion allows GridMarket to model the idea that flexibility can have different value in different locations.

A battery in one zone may therefore have a different modeled opportunity than an equivalent battery elsewhere.

## 9. Predictive Analysis Tool

Predictive analytics should be a **core product feature**, not an optional visualization.

The tool should help a user answer:

> **When is flexibility likely to become more valuable, and why?**

### 9.1 Initial inputs

The predictive engine should consider:

- current real-time price;
- day-ahead price;
- recent price movement;
- expected system/load-zone demand;
- generator outage capacity;
- change in outage capacity;
- congestion;
- binding constraints;
- shadow prices;
- wind forecast;
- solar forecast;
- historical behavior for comparable conditions.

### 9.2 Initial model

For the first implementation, prefer an explainable deterministic or statistical scoring model.

Example conceptual score:

```text
Opportunity Score =
    Price Spread
  + Load Pressure
  + Outage Pressure
  + Congestion Pressure
  + Renewable Deficit
```

Each factor should be normalized before combining them.

Do not present this score as guaranteed profit.

### 9.3 Example output

```text
Houston — 6 PM Flexibility Outlook

Expected scarcity: HIGH
Confidence: 78%

Market:
Current simulated future: $0.19 / credit
Predicted flexibility value: $0.26 / credit

Drivers:
+ Generator outage capacity increasing
+ Binding congestion increasing
+ High forecast demand
+ Solar production falling
- Moderate wind forecast

Interpretation:
Future flexibility may be undervalued relative to the current simulation price.
```

### 9.4 Stretch model

If the platform is stable and time remains:

- train XGBoost/LightGBM on historical ERCOT data;
- compare model output to deterministic score;
- expose feature importance;
- backtest predictions against historical settlement prices.

The model should remain explainable to judges.

## 10. Provider Adapter / Plugin Architecture

GridMarket should not be tied to a single energy company.

A Base-like provider connects to the platform through a normalized provider interface.

Example adapter contract:

```text
getCustomers()
getAssets()
getStateOfCharge()
getAvailableCapacity()
getMinimumReserve()
getChargeLimit()
getDischargeLimit()

reserveCapacity()
releaseCapacity()
verifyDelivery()

getAssetStatus()
```

The provider continues to own:

- customer relationship;
- physical battery;
- firmware;
- telemetry;
- device safety;
- local dispatch authority.

GridMarket owns:

- normalized asset representation;
- market participation;
- order lifecycle;
- prediction;
- simulated settlement;
- user portfolio;
- API access;
- market-level risk controls.

### Weekend provider implementations

Create at least:

```text
providers/
  base-simulator/
  generic-bess/
```

Stretch:

```text
  solar-plus-storage-simulator/
```

This demonstrates that the market is a platform rather than a one-off Base application.

## 12. Algorithms and AI Agents

GridMarket should support a progression of automation:

```text
Manual Trading
      ↓
Rules
      ↓
Algorithms
      ↓
AI Agents
```

Every method should ultimately submit orders through the same trading API.

### Example algorithm

```python
prediction = client.predictions(
    zone="LZ_HOUSTON",
    delivery="18:00"
)

if (
    prediction.expected_value > prediction.market_price * 1.20
    and prediction.confidence > 0.75
):
    client.buy(
        symbol="FLEX-LZ_HOUSTON-18",
        quantity=5
    )
```

### Example AI intent

A user could eventually specify:

> Keep my battery above 45%, minimize my expected electricity cost, and use spare capacity when the predicted opportunity is attractive.

The AI must not bypass policy checks.

Flow:

```text
AI / Algorithm
      |
      v
Order Intent
      |
      v
Policy + Risk Engine
      |
      +-- minimum reserve?
      +-- sufficient funds?
      +-- capacity available?
      +-- position limit?
      +-- rate limit?
      +-- user authorization?
      |
      v
Market Engine
```

## 13. Judge / Developer Sandbox

A key demo objective is allowing another developer to interact with the market quickly.

The GitHub repository should contain:

```text
/examples
  /python-trader
  /typescript-trader
  /ai-agent

/sdk
  /python
  /typescript
```

A judge should be able to:

1. obtain a sandbox API key;
2. clone/copy a minimal example;
3. change a few lines;
4. submit an order;
5. immediately see that order appear on the live GridMarket dashboard.

Example:

```python
from gridmarket import Client

client = Client("demo-key")

print(client.market("FLEX-LZ_HOUSTON-18"))

client.buy(
    symbol="FLEX-LZ_HOUSTON-18",
    quantity=2,
    max_price=0.22
)
```

This interaction is one of the highest-value demonstrations of the platform idea.

## 15. Security, Risk, and Adversarial Behavior

Because GridMarket combines energy assets, trading, APIs, and automation, adversarial behavior should be visible in the design.

### Primary threats

| Threat | Example |
|---|---|
| Double commitment | Same battery capacity sold twice |
| Telemetry spoofing | Battery reports more energy than it has |
| Replay | Old valid order or telemetry reused |
| Oracle manipulation | Market signal intentionally corrupted |
| Sybil accounts | Many fake customers manipulate demand |
| Spoofing | Fake orders influence apparent market price |
| Wash trading | Related accounts trade with themselves |
| Race condition | Two trades reserve the same capacity |
| Provider outage | Provider disappears during delivery |
| Bot runaway | Bad algorithm rapidly loses account funds |
| Capacity default | Seller cannot satisfy a promised interval |
| API abuse | Automated client floods market endpoints |

### Required MVP controls

- atomic capacity reservation;
- idempotent order submission;
- order nonce/request IDs;
- minimum battery reserve;
- API rate limits;
- per-account position limits;
- maximum daily simulated loss;
- maximum order size;
- kill switch;
- provider health state;
- append-only audit trail;
- anomaly flags;
- market halt capability.

## 20. Demo Narrative

The demonstration should tell one cohesive story.

### Step 1 — The market is alive

Show dozens or hundreds of simulated customers with:

- different providers;
- battery capacities;
- reserve requirements;
- locations;
- trading strategies.

Every account begins with $1,000 of simulated trading funds.

### Step 2 — Real ERCOT conditions

Show:

- real-time price;
- day-ahead price;
- expected load;
- generator outages;
- congestion;
- renewable forecast.

### Step 3 — Prediction

Example:

```text
Houston 6 PM

Generator outages: rising
Congestion: increasing
Forecast demand: high
Solar: declining

Predicted scarcity: 81%
Expected flexibility value: $0.27
Current simulated future: $0.20
```

### Step 4 — Traders react

Simulated users and algorithms begin buying the future product.

The order book changes live.

### Step 5 — Automated trading

Show the first-party bot using the exact same public API available to anyone else.

### Step 6 — Provider interoperability

Show customers from two provider adapters trading on the same exchange.

### Step 7 — Judge/developer interaction

A judge runs:

```python
client.buy("FLEX-LZ_HOUSTON-18", quantity=2)
```

Their order appears live in the UI.

### Step 8 — Adversarial event

A simulated participant tries to:

- sell more capacity than available; or
- submit replayed orders; or
- cause an abnormal automated trading burst.

The risk engine rejects or contains the action.

This demonstrates that GridMarket is not merely a dashboard; it is a market, platform, API, orchestration layer, and security boundary.

## 22. Success Criteria

The MVP is successful if:

1. Real ERCOT information is visibly driving the simulation.
2. Generated users begin with $1,000 and can trade.
3. Spot and future flexibility products function.
4. Outages and congestion affect predictive analysis.
5. Predictions provide an explanation, not just a number.
6. Multiple simulated provider fleets can participate.
7. External clients can use the API.
8. A developer can submit a trade from a short example program.
9. Automated strategies use the same API as humans.
10. The system prevents basic unsafe or inconsistent trades.
11. The UI tells a coherent story without requiring a long explanation.
